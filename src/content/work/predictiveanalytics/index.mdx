For my Predictive Analytics course, I built a classification model in Google Colab that predicts which of five drugs suits a patient, based on their age, sex, blood pressure, cholesterol level, and sodium-to-potassium ratio.

## The dataset

The data contains 200 patient records, each labeled with one of five drug types: Amlodipine, Amoxicillin, Atenolol, Ciprofloxacin, or Simvastatin. There were no missing values, so the work focused on preparing the features for modeling.

## What I did

- **Prepared the data:** encoded sex as 0 and 1, one-hot encoded blood pressure and cholesterol, and converted the drug labels into numbers.
- **Split it three ways:** 60% training, 20% validation, and 20% test, stratified so every set keeps the same mix of drugs.
- **Built two models:** Gaussian Naive Bayes, and k-Nearest Neighbors with scaled features.
- **Tuned k-NN:** tested odd values of k from 1 to 15 using 3-fold cross-validation scored on weighted F1.
- **Evaluated both models:** accuracy, precision, recall, F1, and confusion matrices across training, validation, and test sets.

k-NN scored higher on the validation set, so I selected it as the final model. On the test set, the two models ended up very close.

## Key takeaways

- **k = 1 memorizes the training data.** It scored 100% on training data but dropped on unseen data, a classic sign of overfitting.
- **Small test sets are noisy.** With only 40 test patients, the 2.5-point gap between the models is a single patient, so the difference isn't strong evidence for either model.
- **Accuracy hides weak spots.** Both models found Amlodipine, the most common drug, harder to recall, and often confused it with other drugs.
- **Next steps I would try:** larger values of k, other models such as decision trees or random forests, and cross-validation across the full dataset for a more stable comparison.
